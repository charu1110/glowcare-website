The oldest money is the least collected

Collection normally improves with age. This book runs backwards — July, the oldest month, collects worst; August, far more recent, collects best. That pattern means old bills are not being chased, and July alone is now 53.6% of everything owed.

Lifting July to August's rate would release about ₹83,000 in cash without selling one extra dozen.

Data quality

What was fixed, what is still missing

The raw ledger's arithmetic was sound — qty × rate = amount held in all 100 bills. What was broken was everything around the numbers.

Fixed in the cleaning pass

  • 2Duplicate bills — 1011 and 1042 entered twice, inflating the book by ₹18,900.
  • 83Wrong weekdays of 100. The day column was decorative; now computed from the date.
  • 4Date formats in one column, mixing day-first and month-first. 19 dates had no year.
  • 41Money cells stored as text — Rs 2160, ₹5400, 4800/-.
  • 44→12Buyer spellings collapsed to real buyers; 26→8 for products.
  • 16Blank cells refilled from qty × rate, which held wherever it could be checked.

Still not recorded anywhere

  • costCost per dozen. No margin, no profit — you cannot tell whether a 31% udhaar rate is survivable or fatal.
  • whenPayment date. You see that a bill was paid, never when. Collection days are unmeasurable.
  • termsCredit terms. No due date, no agreed limit — so no bill is ever formally overdue.
  • stockDelivery status. Order and dispatch share one row; disputes and returns are invisible.
  • rateAny discount. Every buyer pays an identical rate on all 8 lines across 100 bills — so discounts are verbal and unrecorded.
  • 11Bills dated in the future (₹67,540, up to 27 Sep). Entry errors or advance orders — the ledger cannot say.

Action plan

Ranked by cash released

Ordered by the rupees each one frees, not by how easy it is. The first two need no new customers, no new stock and no price change.

  1. Now

    Put a credit limit on the two buyers holding 43% of the debt

    Kapoor & Sons (₹52,560 owed, 33.7% collected) and Singla Textiles (₹39,960, 42.2%) together hold ₹92,520 while being the two worst payers. Kapoor & Sons is the largest buyer by billing — which is exactly how the exposure grew unnoticed.

    Set a rupee ceiling per buyer and one rule: no new dispatch while the ceiling is breached.

    At stake ₹92,520 42.6% of receivables, 2 buyers
  2. Now

    Work the July book before it ages out

    ₹1,16,326 of July billing is unpaid — over half of everything owed, in the month with the worst collection rate. Nothing here has passed 90 days, so none of it has gone stale yet.

    Work it oldest-first, not largest-first: the 61–90 day bracket alone is ₹69,056 across 11 bills, so eleven phone calls cover a third of the problem.

    At stake ₹1,16,326 53.6% of receivables, 34 bills
  3. Next

    Take part-advance on the lines that never get paid for

    Collection varies enormously by line, and not randomly. Sweater collects 100%. Vest collects 22.9%, Thermal 37.5%, Lower 50.2% — winter stock leaving on udhaar before the season turns.

    Ask for part-advance on those three specifically. Leave sweater and t-shirt terms alone; they already work.

    At stake ₹87,046 Unpaid on Vest, Thermal, Lower
  4. Next

    Add four columns to the bill book

    Cost per dozen, payment date, due date, delivery status. Cost matters most: on ₹6,99,040 of billing you cannot currently state one rupee of profit.

    Payment date is the cheapest to add and unlocks the most — it turns paid/unpaid into a real collection-days figure per buyer.

    Unlocks Margin Unmeasurable on the full ₹6.99L
  5. Watch

    Reduce reliance on five buyers

    The top 5 buyers are 55.4% of revenue, the top 3 are 35.3%. A median of 6 days between a buyer's orders means these are close working relationships — a strength, until one stops.

    New Punjab Cloth House averages ₹3,480 a bill against a book average of ₹6,990; growing small existing accounts is cheaper than finding new ones.

    Concentration 55.4% Revenue from 5 of 12 buyers
  6. Watch

    Fix date entry at the source

    11 bills are dated after 10 September, and 6 more are genuinely undecidable because the ledger mixes day-first and month-first in one column. Writing dates one way — DD-MM-YYYY, always with the year — costs nothing and removes an error no cleaning can fully undo.

    Affected 17 bills ₹67,540 future-dated, 6 ambiguous
